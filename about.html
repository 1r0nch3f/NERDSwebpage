How I Work

A good PC should fit the person using it, not the other way around. I take the time to understand what you actually need, recommend parts that make sense for your budget, and build something that lasts. No filler, no pressure, and no parts you will never use.

Why No Storefront?Savings you can see

I've run a storefront before, and here's what I learned: retail isn't what it used to be, and every dollar of rent, power, insurance and counter hours ends up baked into your bill. In Dalton, even a modest 1,000 square foot shop runs about $1,600 to $2,400 a month before anyone is hired to keep the doors open. Add one part-time person to keep regular hours and it's $3,500 to $4,750 a month. Spread across a normal month of jobs, that's roughly $25 to $80 added to every repair.

I'd rather keep that money in your pocket. So I work by appointment: drop it off, let me in remotely, or I come to you. Same work, same warranty, without paying for a lobby you'd spend five minutes in.

And no storefront doesn't mean a one-man bottleneck. I have a crew of techs I've worked with over the years who are happy to step in when a job needs more hands.

Who I Am

I have been building and repairing computers for years, have earned more than ten industry certifications along the way, and taught the next round of techs as an adjunct professor at Chattanooga State, where I am still on the adjunct roster. NERDS PC is local and hands-on, but my builds have shipped all over the world. Most of my work comes from word of mouth, and that keeps me honest.
